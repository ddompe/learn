## Why this matters

Computers are very good at arithmetic, but not always in the way you expect. With
decimals, tiny errors appear. In a report that adds thousands of amounts, a hidden
error can make a total differ by a cent from the one in the ledger. That kind of
mismatch costs hours to track down.

<CaseStudy>
	Café Central records each sale in colones, with cents. Lucía adds up 240 sales
	and gets a total one cent different from the accounting system. Nothing is
	wrong with her formula. The cause is how numbers are stored.
</CaseStudy>

## Integers and floats

An **integer** is a whole number: 3, 150, -7. Computers store integers exactly, up to
a very large limit.

A **float** (floating-point number) holds a number with a decimal point, such as 2.5 or
0.1. To fit in a fixed amount of space, the computer stores a close approximation in
binary (base 2), the same way you cannot write one third exactly as a decimal.

Just as 1/3 is 0.3333... in decimal, 0.1 is an endless pattern in binary. The computer
cuts it off, and a tiny error results. Usually it is invisible. Sometimes it is not.

Excel uses floats too. It hides most of the error by rounding what it displays.

## Exact decimals

For money, we need numbers that behave like decimals on paper. Many languages offer an
exact decimal type. In Python, it is called `Decimal`. It stores digits as digits, so
`0.1` is exactly one tenth.

Decimals are slower than floats, but for business data the difference does not matter.
A good rule: **use floats for measurements, and decimals for money.**

## Common mistakes

- **Using floats for money.** Use decimals, or whole cents stored as integers.
- **Comparing floats for equality.** `0.1 + 0.2 == 0.3` is false. Compare with a small
  tolerance instead.
- **Creating a Decimal from a float.** Use text: `Decimal("0.1")`.
- **Trusting the display.** A cell showing 0.30 may hold 0.30000000000000004.
- **Mixed formats in the data.** The Café Central file writes some amounts as `$1500,50`.
  You must clean them before doing arithmetic. Parts 5 and 6 show how.

## Why this matters

A chart is a claim. People remember the picture and forget the numbers. A chart can mislead without
a single false number, just through the choices of the axis, the type, or the time range.
Doing it by accident is as harmful as doing it on purpose. Your reports will be trusted
more if the charts are honest.

## Ways charts mislead

1. **A truncated axis.** Bars encode value by **length**, so the axis must start at zero.
   Cutting it exaggerates differences.
2. **A cherry-picked time range.** Starting at a peak or a trough tells a different story.
3. **Pie charts with many slices,** or 3D effects. Angles and depth are hard to compare.
4. **Dual axes** with different scales, which let you make any two lines look related.
5. **Unlabelled axes and units.** "Sales" in what? Colones, thousands of colones?
6. **Unordered bars.** Sort categories by value unless they have a natural order.
7. **Colour that carries no meaning,** or colour alone to separate categories for colour-blind readers.

## A checklist before sharing

1. Does the chart answer a **question** that is written in the title?
2. Do bars start at **zero**?
3. Are the **units** and the **period** shown?
4. Are categories **sorted** sensibly?
5. Would the message change if I chose a different time range?
6. Can someone who is colour-blind read it?
7. Does the number in the chart match the table it came from?

## Common mistakes

- **Choosing the chart before the question.**
- **Defaults that truncate.** Some tools start the axis at the lowest value. Check.
- **Decorating.** Shadows, 3D, and gradients add nothing.
- **Hiding the data source or the period.**
- **Trusting an AI-generated chart without reading the axis.**

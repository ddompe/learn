## Why this matters

Cleaning is where most of the time in data work goes, and where silent mistakes are made. A
careless cleaning step can drop rows, change numbers, or merge things that should stay
apart, and nobody will notice. The discipline of this lesson is: change data
deliberately, log every change, and verify the result.

## The cleaning module

The functions from lessons 4.9 and 4.12 are reused. They are gathered in a module that returns the clean table and a log.

<Example file="part06/cafe_clean.py" />

Why each step is done that way:

1. **Read as text** (`dtype=str`, `keep_default_na=False`). pandas does not guess types or turn empty
   fields into missing values. You decide.
2. **Remove exact duplicates** with `drop_duplicates()`. Only identical rows go. Two sales with the same
   ID but different amounts would stay, and a person should look at them.
3. **Convert dates** with `parse_date`, because the formats are mixed, and the slash style is day first (lesson 4.12).
4. **Convert amounts** to `Decimal`, for exact money (lesson 1.8).
5. **Normalise categories** with `simplify`, so `Café`, `cafe`, and `café` become `cafe`.
6. **Mark missing customers** as `SIN-CLIENTE` rather than dropping the rows. They are real sales, and the total must still tie.

And notice what the function returns: the **log**, a list of what it did.

<Example file="part06/04_cleaning.py" />

The run reports 246 rows read, 6 duplicates removed, 7 rows with no customer marked, and 240 rows in
the result. The types are now dates, `Decimal` amounts, and text.

## Rules of good cleaning

1. **Never overwrite the original.** Read from the raw file, and write results elsewhere.
2. **Log what you change,** with counts. A surprise in the counts is a signal.
3. **Prefer marking over deleting.** Keep the row, flag the problem.
4. **Make rules explicit.** "Dates with slashes are day first" is a rule. Write it down.
5. **Check the totals** before and after. Removing duplicates should reduce the count by exactly the number reported.
6. **Don't chain changes you can't explain.** Each step should have a reason.

## Common mistakes

- **Overwriting the original file.**
- **Dropping rows to make errors disappear.**
- **Cleaning without counting.** You can't tell what changed.
- **Using floats for money.**
- **Guessing the date order.** Check it against unambiguous values.
- **Assuming that duplicates are always errors.** Two identical sales can happen. Here the whole line, including the ID, repeats.

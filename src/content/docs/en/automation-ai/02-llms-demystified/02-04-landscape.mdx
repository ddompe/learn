## Why this matters

People say "ChatGPT", "Copilot", and "Claude" as if they were the same kind of thing.
They are not, and the confusion causes bad decisions: choosing the wrong tool, pasting
data into the wrong place, or reading a comparison that mixes categories. This lesson
gives you three words that keep them apart. Names, features, and prices in this
market change quickly, so the lesson teaches the structure and tells you where to look
up the details.

## Three layers

<Mermaid
	diagram={`flowchart TB
  A["Product: the app you use, such as a chat site or an editor extension"]
  B["Model: the trained LLM that writes the answers"]
  C["Vendor: the company that built and runs the model"]
  A --> B --> C`}
/>

- A **vendor** is a company that trains models. Examples include OpenAI, Anthropic, and
  Google.
- A **model** is a specific trained LLM, usually part of a **family** that has small,
  fast versions and large, capable ones. Models are updated and retired often.
- A **product** is an app that you use, which calls a model behind the scenes. Products
  add features: a chat window, search, file upload, saved notes, and integration with
  other tools.

Two facts follow:

1. **One product can offer several models,** and may change which one it uses without
   much notice.
2. **One model can power several products.** A vendor's own chat app and a third party's
   tool may both call the same model. Their rules for your data may differ.

For this course, the AI assistant is GitHub Copilot in VS Code (Part 3). Copilot is a
product. The models inside it come from several vendors and can change.

## Closed and open models

Most well-known models are **closed**: you use them through the vendor's service. Some
are **open-weight**, meaning the vendor publishes the files so you can run them yourself.
Lesson 2.5 compares them.

## How to compare tools sensibly

Ask these questions, in this order:

1. Which product am I using, and which model is it calling?
2. Which plan am I on? Free, personal paid, and company plans can treat data differently.
3. Can it search the web, read my files, or use other tools?
4. What are the limits and costs?
5. What does the vendor do with my data? (Lesson 2.8.)

## Common mistakes

- **Comparing a product with a model.** "Copilot versus a model family" is not a fair
  comparison.
- **Assuming the free and paid versions behave alike.** Features, limits, and privacy can
  differ.
- **Trusting old comparison articles.** In this field, last year's ranking is often out of date.
- **Believing the model name answers everything.** Behaviour also depends on the product's
  instructions and tools.

## Ask your AI assistant

	<Fragment slot="lousy">Which AI is the best?</Fragment>

	<Fragment slot="good">

	<Fragment slot="whyItWorks">
		It uses the vendor, model, and product split, asks for a data-handling

	</Fragment>

		assistant marks as uncertain as unknown.
	</Fragment>

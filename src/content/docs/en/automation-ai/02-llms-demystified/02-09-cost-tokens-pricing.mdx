## Why this matters

Many assistants feel free, and a free plan can be enough to learn with. But when a
company automates work with AI, or when you use paid plans, costs appear, and they can
surprise you. A small estimate before a big job avoids a nasty bill. The prices
themselves change often, so this lesson teaches the method and shows you where to find
current numbers.

<CaseStudy>
	Café Central wants to summarise every month's sales file automatically.
	Daniela is asked how much that might cost per year.
</CaseStudy>

## Why per token

Remember that the model reads and writes tokens (lesson 2.1). The work it does,
and the computers it needs, grow with the number of tokens. So services charge by tokens
processed, usually per million. They charge differently for:

- **Input tokens:** everything you send, including the conversation so far and pasted files.
- **Output tokens:** what the model writes. These usually cost more per token.

Some services add cheaper rates for repeated input, or for work that can wait. Check the
vendor's page for details.

Also, subscriptions such as monthly plans hide token prices behind a fixed fee and
usage limits.

## A simple estimate

1. Estimate the **input** size in tokens. A rough rule for English: characters divided by 4.
   Spanish often needs more.
2. Estimate the **output** size.
3. Multiply each by its price per million tokens, divided by one million.
4. Multiply by how many times you will do it.

## What makes a task more expensive

- **A longer input:** big files, long chats resent every time (lesson 2.3).
- **A longer output:** a short answer costs less than a long report.
- **A more capable model:** large models cost more per token than small ones.
- **Repetition:** a job run on thousands of files multiplies everything.
- **Retries and testing:** while building an automation, you will run the job many times.

## Hands-on

### Your turn

Using the example rates in the script ($3 per million input tokens and $15 per million
output tokens), estimate the cost of a job with 100,000 input tokens and 2,000 output tokens.

<Checkpoint question="Show the answer">
	Input: 100,000 times 3 divided by 1,000,000 is 0.30 dollars. Output: 2,000
	times 15 divided by 1,000,000 is 0.03 dollars. The total is 0.33 dollars.
</Checkpoint>

## Common mistakes

- **Forgetting that input includes the whole conversation.** Long chats get costly.
- **Using today's price for next year's plan.** Prices and models change.
- **Comparing prices without comparing quality.** A cheaper model that needs retries may cost more.
- **Ignoring limits on subscription plans.** "Unlimited" often has fair-use limits.
- **Testing on the whole data set.** Test on a few rows first.

## Ask your AI assistant

<PromptExample>
	<Fragment slot="lousy">How much will this cost?</Fragment>
	<Fragment slot="whatGoesWrong">
		The assistant names a price from memory, likely out of date, and does not
		show how it got it.
	</Fragment>
	<Fragment slot="good">
		I plan to summarise one CSV file of about 8,000 characters each month, with
		an output of about 500 tokens. I will give you the input and output prices
		per million tokens from my vendor's pricing page: input 3.00 dollars, output
		15.00 dollars. Show the calculation step by step in a table, for one run and
		for twelve runs. Do not use any price I have not given you.
	</Fragment>
	<Fragment slot="whyItWorks">
		It supplies the **input** (sizes and prices) and a **constraint** that
		forbids remembered prices. The step-by-step **output format** doubles as
		**verification**.
	</Fragment>
	<Fragment slot="check">
		Redo the arithmetic with a calculator or spreadsheet. Confirm that the
		prices in the answer are the ones you supplied, and that the token estimate
		uses about four characters per token.
	</Fragment>
</PromptExample>

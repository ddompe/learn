## Why this matters

Two questions come up in every company: "Is our data safe?" and "What will this cost?".
The answers depend on where the model runs and who controls it. Knowing the options lets
you take part in that conversation and choose with your eyes open.

<CaseStudy>
	The owner of Café Central wants to analyse customer comments, but the comments
	contain names and email addresses. Daniela must find out where such data may
	go.
</CaseStudy>

## Two separate choices

These two choices are often mixed up.

1. **Who controls the model?** Closed or open-weight.
2. **Where does it run?** In the cloud, or on your own computer.

| Choice      | Meaning                                                                                     |
| ----------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Closed      | You use the model only through the vendor's service.                                        |
| Open-weight | The vendor publishes the model files. Anyone may download and run them, within the license. |
| Cloud       | The model runs on the vendor's or a host's servers. Your prompt travels there.              |
| Local       | The model runs on your own machine. Your prompt stays there.                                |

Open-weight does not mean you must run it locally. Many hosting services offer
open-weight models in the cloud. And a closed model cannot be run locally, because
you do not have its files.

The four combinations look like this:

|                 | Cloud                                         | Local                                |
| --------------- | --------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------ |
| **Closed**      | Typical chat apps and vendor services         | Not possible                         |
| **Open-weight** | Hosting services that run open models for you | Running a model on your own computer |

## The trade-offs

**Privacy.** A local model keeps your data on your machine. A cloud service sends it
away, so you depend on the vendor's terms (lesson 2.8). Company plans often include
stronger promises than free plans.

**Cost.** Cloud services charge per use or per month. Local models have no per-use fee,
but you need a capable computer, and your time to set it up.

**Capability.** The strongest models tend to be large and closed, and run in the cloud.
Models small enough to run on a laptop are improving, but are usually less capable. For
many everyday tasks, a smaller model is enough.

**Effort.** A cloud product works in minutes. A local setup takes more skill, and on a
locked-down company laptop may not be allowed.

## Hands-on

### Your turn

Café Central wants to summarise 200 customer comments that include real names. Choose
an option, and give one reason.

1. A free consumer chat site.
2. A company-approved cloud plan with a data-protection agreement.
3. A small open-weight model running on the office laptop.

<Checkpoint question="Show a sample answer">
	Option 1 is the weakest, because the data leaves the company under consumer
	terms. Options 2 and 3 can both be acceptable. Option 2 is more capable and
	easy. Option 3 keeps data on the laptop, but may give weaker summaries and
	needs setup. The company's policy decides.
</Checkpoint>

## Common mistakes

- **Equating "open" with "private".** An open-weight model hosted by someone else still
  sends your data away.
- **Equating "cloud" with "unsafe".** A contract can make a cloud service safer than an
  unmanaged laptop.
- **Installing local tools without permission.** Check company policy first.
- **Expecting a laptop model to match the largest cloud models.**

## Why this matters

Pasting data into a chat is easy and feels private. It is not always. The text goes to
servers run by another company. Depending on the plan and the settings, it may be
stored, reviewed by people, or used to train future models. Sharing the wrong data can
break privacy law, a contract, or your employer's rules, and it cannot be undone.

## Kinds of data

| Kind                     | Examples at Café Central                  | Risk                            |
| ------------------------ | ----------------------------------------- | ------------------------------- |
| Public                   | The menu, opening hours                   | Low                             |
| Internal                 | Monthly sales totals, internal procedures | Medium. Check the policy.       |
| Personal data            | Customer names, emails, phone numbers     | High. Laws and contracts apply. |
| Secrets                  | Passwords, API keys, bank details         | Never share                     |
| Confidential by contract | A supplier's price list under NDA         | High. The contract decides.     |

**Personal data** is information that identifies a person. Many countries regulate it.

## Consumer versus enterprise plans

The same vendor often sells different plans with different promises.

- **Consumer plans** (free or personal) are for individuals. By default, your
  conversations may be kept, and may be used to improve models, unless you turn that
  off. Settings and terms differ between vendors and change over time.
- **Business and enterprise plans** usually include written commitments: data is not used
  to train models, access is controlled, and retention is limited. Your company signs a
  contract for these.

You cannot tell from the product name which plan applies. Check the account.

## Practical rules

1. **Find your company's AI policy.** If there is none, ask your manager or IT.
2. **Use the company-approved tool** for work data.
3. **Share the minimum.** Replace names with codes (C001), and remove emails you do not
   need. This is called **anonymising**. Be careful: a few details together can still
   identify a person.
4. **Never paste secrets,** whatever the plan.
5. **Check the settings** for chat history and training on your data.
6. **When unsure, do not paste.** Describe the structure of your data instead.

## Common mistakes

- **Assuming "private mode" means private.** It usually only affects your history.
- **Using a personal account for work data.** Your company's protections do not apply.
- **Anonymising by deleting names only.** Emails, cities, and rare details can still identify people.
- **Pasting a whole file when five rows would do.**
- **Assuming the policy will not change.** Terms of services are updated.

## Check yourself

<Checkpoint question="Name two kinds of data you must never or rarely paste into a consumer chat.">
	Secrets, such as passwords and keys, and personal or contract-confidential
	data.
</Checkpoint>

<Checkpoint question="Why can the same vendor's consumer and enterprise plans differ?">
	Enterprise plans come with a contract that often limits retention and forbids
	training on your data. Consumer plans follow default terms.
</Checkpoint>

<Checkpoint question="What does anonymising mean?">
	Removing or replacing details that identify a person, such as swapping a name
	for a code.
</Checkpoint>
